Brain tumour delineation

In lecture 0 we saw this task: before radiotherapy, someone draws the tumour on the scan, slice by slice.

We also saw a short program that does it. This lecture explains that program: the logic its words come from, how a formula is computed on an image, and how the implementation has changed since 2019.

A logic and a model checker

A spatial logic is used as a description language. A model checker is its interpreter.

Together they give a declarative domain-specific language for graphs, images and 3D meshes.

  • A formula describes a property of points: "dark, and touching the border".
  • A model is a space, with facts about its points: an image and its colours.
  • The model checker computes the set of points where the formula holds: for an image, another image.

The same shape as evaluate(expression, environment) in lecture 4: a syntax, a semantic domain, and a function from one to the other.

One logic, several kinds of space

  • Reachability in a maze.
  • Annotation of maps.
  • GPS traces of moving vehicles.
  • Medical images, in 2D and 3D.

The formulas are the same in each case. What changes is the space: a graph, a grid of pixels, a grid of voxels.

A sentence we would like to evaluate

All the purple dogs will turn red, unless they are no more than 7 metres from the region where all the pink elephants will be tomorrow.

To give it a meaning we must decide a few things first.

spaceIs it discrete or continuous?
metricIs there a distance, so that "7 metres" means something?
factsWhat are the atomic facts: "purple", "dog"?
timeIs time involved, and how?

These are choices of a semantic domain. In this lecture the domain comes first, the syntax second, the interpreter third.

Modal logic

\( \Phi ::= p \mid \neg \Phi \mid \Phi \wedge \Phi \mid \Diamond \Phi \)

A Kripke frame is a set of worlds \(W\) with a relation \(R\). A model adds which atomic propositions hold in which world.

\( x \models \Diamond\Phi \iff \exists y.\; x\,R\,y \wedge y \models \Phi \)

\(\Diamond\Phi\) is read "possibly \(\Phi\)". Its dual is \(\Box\Phi = \neg\Diamond\neg\Phi\), "necessarily \(\Phi\)".

The same syntax can be read on other structures. In 1944 McKinsey and Tarski read it on topological spaces.

Topological spaces

A topological space is a set \(X\) with a family of subsets called open: it contains \(\emptyset\) and \(X\), and it is closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.

A set is closed when its complement is open.

The closure \(\mathcal{C}(A)\) is the smallest closed set containing \(A\): \(A\) together with its boundary.

The interior \(\mathcal{I}(A)\) is the largest open set contained in \(A\).

\( \mathcal{I}(A) = X \setminus \mathcal{C}(X \setminus A) \)

Kuratowski's closure axioms

A topology can be given by its closure operator alone. An operator \(\mathcal{C}\) on the subsets of \(X\) is the closure of a topology exactly when

\( \mathcal{C}(\emptyset) = \emptyset \)nothing is close to the empty set
\( A \subseteq \mathcal{C}(A) \)every point is close to itself
\( \mathcal{C}(A \cup B) = \mathcal{C}(A) \cup \mathcal{C}(B) \)closure goes through unions
\( \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}(A)) = \mathcal{C}(A) \)closing twice adds nothing

The closed sets are then the sets with \(\mathcal{C}(A) = A\).

McKinsey and Tarski, 1944

Read \(\Diamond\) as closure and \(\Box\) as interior.

\( ⟦\Diamond\Phi⟧ = \mathcal{C}(⟦\Phi⟧) \qquad ⟦\Box\Phi⟧ = \mathcal{I}(⟦\Phi⟧) \)

  • A formula denotes a set of points: \(⟦p⟧\) is where \(p\) holds, \(⟦\neg\Phi⟧\) is the complement, \(⟦\Phi \wedge \Psi⟧\) the intersection.
  • \(\Diamond\Phi\) holds at the points that are close to \(\Phi\). \(\Box\Phi\) holds at the points well inside \(\Phi\).
  • Kuratowski's axioms, read as formulas, correspond to the axioms of the modal logic S4.

J. C. C. McKinsey, A. Tarski. The Algebra of Topology. Annals of Mathematics 45(1), 1944.

Exercise: a lollipop

In the plane, red holds on an open disc (its circle excluded) and on a segment that touches the disc at one point.

Where do these hold?

  • \(\Diamond\,\)red
  • \(\Diamond\,\)red \(\wedge \neg\Box\,\)red
  • \(\Diamond\Box\,\)red
  • red \(\wedge \neg\Diamond\Box\,\)red

Exercise: the answers

Dashed: the circle is excluded. A thin segment has no interior, so \(\Box\,\)red loses it. The last formula holds exactly on the segment, without the point where it touches the disc.

From topology to images

On a grid of pixels, the natural closure of a set adds the pixels one step away from it.

Applied twice, it adds the pixels two steps away. So \(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}(A)) \neq \mathcal{C}(A)\): the last of Kuratowski's axioms fails, and a grid with this closure is not a topological space.

Čech studied closure operators without that axiom. A closure space is a set \(X\) with an operator \(\mathcal{C}\) such that

\( \mathcal{C}(\emptyset) = \emptyset \qquad A \subseteq \mathcal{C}(A) \)
\( \mathcal{C}(A \cup B) = \mathcal{C}(A) \cup \mathcal{C}(B) \)

Topological spaces are the closure spaces where closure is idempotent.

E. Čech. Topological Spaces. Wiley, 1966.

Closure from a relation

Every relation \(R\) on a set \(X\) gives a closure: a set, plus every point that one of its points is related to.

\( \mathcal{C}_R(A) = A \cup \{\, x \mid \exists a \in A.\; a \mathrel{R} x \,\} \)

These are the quasi-discrete closure spaces. Every graph is one, and the closure is not idempotent.

An image is a graph

The pixels are the points. Each pixel is related to the pixels around it.

In 2D, VoxLogicA relates a pixel to its 8 neighbours; in 3D, a voxel to its 26 neighbours.

The atomic facts are facts about one point: "its intensity is above 0.95", "it is blue".

A closure model

Each point is related to the points above, below, left and right of it. The atomic propositions are the colours. The next slides use this grid.

The Spatial Logic of Closure Spaces

\( \Phi ::= p \mid \top \mid \neg \Phi \mid \Phi \wedge \Phi \mid \mathcal{N}\,\Phi \mid \Phi\,\mathcal{S}\,\Psi \)

\( p,\ \top,\ \neg,\ \wedge \)an atomic proposition, true, negation, conjunction
\( \mathcal{N}\,\Phi \)near \(\Phi\): the closure of the points of \(\Phi\)
\( \Phi\,\mathcal{S}\,\Psi \)\(\Phi\) surrounded by \(\Psi\)

Disjunction, interior, boundary and reachability are derived from these.

V. Ciancia, D. Latella, M. Loreti, M. Massink. Model Checking Spatial Logics for Closure Spaces. Logical Methods in Computer Science 12(4), 2016.

Each formula denotes a set of points

\( ⟦p⟧ = \mathcal{V}(p) \)where the valuation says \(p\) holds
\( ⟦\top⟧ = X \)every point
\( ⟦\neg\Phi⟧ = X \setminus ⟦\Phi⟧ \)complement
\( ⟦\Phi \wedge \Psi⟧ = ⟦\Phi⟧ \cap ⟦\Psi⟧ \)intersection
\( ⟦\mathcal{N}\,\Phi⟧ = \mathcal{C}(⟦\Phi⟧) \)closure
\( ⟦\Phi\,\mathcal{S}\,\Psi⟧ \)defined with paths: next slides

Each case is one operation on sets, except the last.

Near

The ringed points satisfy \(\mathcal{N}\,\)red: the red points, and the points one step away from a red point.

Exercise: which red points satisfy \(\mathcal{N}\,\)yellow?

Answer: red \(\wedge\, \mathcal{N}\,\)yellow

The red points with a yellow neighbour. The corners of the red frame have none: they touch yellow only diagonally, and on this grid diagonal points are not related.

Surrounded

\(x\) satisfies \(\Phi\,\mathcal{S}\,\Psi\) when \(x\) is in \(\Phi\), and every path from \(x\) that leaves \(\Phi\) passes through \(\Psi\) first.

\( x \in ⟦\Phi\,\mathcal{S}\,\Psi⟧ \iff x \in ⟦\Phi⟧ \;\wedge\; \forall \pi, \ell.\; \big(\pi(0) = x \wedge \pi(\ell) \notin ⟦\Phi⟧\big) \Rightarrow \exists j.\; 0 < j \le \ell \wedge \pi(j) \in ⟦\Psi⟧ \)

  • A path is a sequence of points, each related to the next. In a general closure space, a path is a continuous function from \(\mathbb{N}\).
  • It is a spatial version of the temporal operator until: you are in \(\Phi\), and you cannot get out of \(\Phi\) without stepping on \(\Psi\).

yellow \(\mathcal{S}\) red

Every yellow point satisfies it: the red frame closes the yellow region on all four sides.

Exercise: green \(\mathcal{S}\) violet?

The violet frame has a gap on the right.

Answer: no green point

From every green point there is a path that walks along the green region to the gap and leaves it on a white point. One gap is enough.

Exercise: red \(\mathcal{S}\) white?

No red point. Walking along the frame, every red point reaches a red point next to yellow, and yellow is neither red nor white.

Reachability

\(x\) satisfies \(\rho\,\Phi[\Psi]\) when there is a path from \(x\) to a point of \(\Phi\), whose intermediate points all satisfy \(\Psi\).

\( \Phi\,\mathcal{S}\,\Psi \;\equiv\; \Phi \wedge \neg\, \rho\, \neg(\Phi \vee \Psi)\,[\neg\Psi] \)

Not surrounded: a way out of \(\Phi\) to a point that is neither \(\Phi\) nor \(\Psi\), without meeting \(\Psi\).

Later papers take reachability as the primitive and derive surrounded. On directed graphs there are two reachability operators, one per direction; a distance operator is also added.

Reachability: TACAS 2019. See also Bartocci, Bortolussi, Loreti, Nenzi, MEMOCODE 2017.

Derived operators are written in the language


let !(a) = not(a)
let N(a) = near(a)
let I(a) = interior(a)
let ~>(a,b) = through(b,a)
let mayReach(a,b) = N((b ~> N a) | a) // This is the \rho operator from TACAS19
let touch(a,b) = a ~> N b
let grow(a,b) = (a | touch(b,a))
let surrounded(a,b) = a \ mayReach(not(a | b),not(b))
let T(a,b) = touch(a,b)
let S(a,b) = surrounded(a,b)

From stdlib.imgql, the library of VoxLogicA 1. touch(a, b) holds at the points of a from which a path inside a reaches a point near b. grow(a, b) is a, plus the parts of b that touch it.

Distance

When the space has a metric, the logic can talk about distance: the points at distance less than 5 from a black point.

On images the Euclidean distance transform gives, for every pixel, its distance from a set. It is computed in time linear in the number of pixels. On general graphs, a variant of Dijkstra's algorithm takes \(O(n \log n)\).

C. R. Maurer, R. Qi, V. Raghavan. IEEE TPAMI 25(2), 2003.

Global model checking

For every sub-formula, compute the set of points where it holds, from the atomic propositions up. Each line below is one sub-formula over a graph of nine points.

Each line is a boolean mask; on an image, a black and white image. This is dynamic programming over the syntax of the formula.

A model checker is an interpreter


def evaluate(phi: Formula, m: Model) -> frozenset[Point]:
    match phi:
        case Atom(name):
            return m.valuation[name]
        case Not(arg):
            return m.points - evaluate(arg, m)
        case And(left, right):
            return evaluate(left, m) & evaluate(right, m)
        case Or(left, right):
            return evaluate(left, m) | evaluate(right, m)
        case Near(arg):
            return near(evaluate(arg, m), m)
        case Surrounded(left, right):
            return surrounded(evaluate(left, m),
                              evaluate(right, m), m)
        case Touch(left, right):
            return touch(evaluate(left, m),
                         evaluate(right, m), m)

The same structure as the evaluator of lecture 4.

The values are sets of points instead of numbers. The model plays the role of the environment.

The whole model checker, with a parser for formulas and a reader for PNG images, is code/lesson-11/slcs.py.

Models


type Point = tuple[int, int]

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Model:
    points: frozenset[Point]
    neighbours: dict[Point, frozenset[Point]]
    valuation: dict[str, frozenset[Point]]

def near(a: frozenset[Point], m: Model) -> frozenset[Point]:
    """The closure of a: a, and every point one step away from it."""
    return a | {y for x in a for y in m.neighbours[x]}

A model is a graph whose points carry atomic propositions. image_model builds one from a PNG: one point per pixel, 8 neighbours each, one atomic proposition per colour.

Surrounded: flooding from the outside


def surrounded(a, b, m):
    good = set(a)
    leaks = {y for x in a
               for y in m.neighbours[x]} - a - b
    while leaks:
        lost = {x for y in leaks
                  for x in m.neighbours[y]} & good
        good -= lost
        leaks = lost - b
    return frozenset(good)

The definition speaks about all paths. The algorithm does not enumerate them.

  • A leak is a point next to a that is in neither a nor b.
  • A point of a next to a leak is lost.
  • A lost point that is not in b is a leak in its turn.

Every point is lost at most once, so the time is linear in the size of the graph.

Flooding green \(\mathcal{S}\) violet

The leak: the white point in the gap of the violet frame, next to green (ringed).

Step 1. The green points next to a leak are lost (crossed); the points lost at this step are the next leaks (ringed).

Step 2. The green points next to a leak are lost (crossed); the points lost at this step are the next leaks (ringed).

Step 3. The green points next to a leak are lost (crossed); the points lost at this step are the next leaks (ringed).

Step 4. The green points next to a leak are lost (crossed); the points lost at this step are the next leaks (ringed).

Step 5. The green points next to a leak are lost (crossed); the points lost at this step are the next leaks (ringed).

No leaks are left, and every green point is lost: green S violet holds nowhere.

Touch: a visit from the target


def touch(a, b, m):
    reached = set(a & near(b, m))
    frontier = set(reached)
    while frontier:
        frontier = {y for x in frontier
                      for y in m.neighbours[x]} & a - reached
        reached |= frontier
    return frozenset(reached)

Start from the points of a that are near b, and visit a from there, breadth first. Each point is visited once.

When the relation is symmetric, as on images, the same set can be computed with connected components: the components of a that contain a point near b. This is how the GPU version of VoxLogicA does it.

VoxLogicA

A spatial model checker for images, in 2D and 3D.

  • Written in F#, on .NET.
  • Image operations from ITK, the Insight Toolkit, through SimpleITK.
  • Reads PNG, BMP, NIfTI and the other formats ITK reads.

Its predecessor, topochecker, was written in OCaml and checked SLCS and its spatio-temporal extension on graphs.

VoxLogicA was presented at TACAS 2019 together with its language, ImgQL.

G. Belmonte, V. Ciancia, D. Latella, M. Massink. VoxLogicA: A Spatial Model Checker for Declarative Image Analysis. TACAS, LNCS 11427, 2019.

Values are whole images

For a model checker, the value of an expression is the set of points where it holds. For an image, that is another image.

A brain scan has about nine million voxels. Every intermediate value of a program is an image of that size, and computing one costs time and memory.

The first design rule of the interpreter: never compute anything twice.

Maximal sharing

The expression i(f(g(x), h(g(x))), h(g(x))) contains g(x) three times and h(g(x)) twice.

Hash the syntax while you build it


table = {}

def node(op, *args):    # args: node ids
    key = (op, args)
    if key not in table:
        table[key] = len(table)
    return table[key]

x = node("x")
gx = node("g", x)
hgx = node("h", gx)
root = node("i", node("f", gx, hgx), hgx)

Asked to build g(x) a second time, node returns the same identifier.

The syntax tree is never built: the source goes straight to a directed acyclic graph with maximal sharing.

Evaluating that graph, each node once, is the global model checking algorithm. The dynamic programming over sub-formulas comes from the data structure.

What was left out

ImgQL has let and functions, and no recursion, no loops, no conditionals on data.

  1. Every function call can be expanded before running, so every program terminates.
  2. The whole computation is a graph before it starts.
  3. Independent nodes of the graph can run in parallel, with no annotation in the source.

Leaving out recursion was a design decision, and these three properties of the implementation follow from it.

Parallel, and on the GPU

On the CPU

The nodes of the graph are tasks. VoxLogicA runs them on all the cores, with dynamic scheduling (the F# library Hopac). Each task is an operation on a whole image, so the overhead of scheduling is small.

On the GPU

VoxLogicA-GPU is a massively parallel implementation. Reachability is computed with connected components, which have good parallel algorithms.

L. Bussi, V. Ciancia, F. Gadducci. FORTE 2021.

The interface

Back to the tumour

Glioblastoma, on a FLAIR scan: the tumour and the swelling around it are brighter than healthy tissue.

The procedure has three steps. Each is a few lines of ImgQL, and each line can be read aloud.

Step 1: remove the background


let background = touch(intensity <. 0.1, border)
let brain = !background

The background is the dark area that touches the border of the image. The brain is the rest.

A plain threshold would also remove the dark areas inside the brain. touch keeps them.

Step 2: thresholds on percentiles


let pflair = percentiles(intensity, brain, 0.5)
let hyperIntense = flt(5.0, pflair >. 0.95)
let veryIntense = flt(2.0, pflair >. 0.86)

Percentiles make the thresholds relative to the intensities of each brain. flt removes the parts narrower than a ball of the given radius. Hyper-intense areas are tumour; very intense areas sometimes are.

Step 3: grow, then texture


let growTum = grow(hyperIntense, veryIntense)
let tumSim = similarTo(growTum)
let tumStatCC = flt(2.0, tumSim >. 0.6)
let gtv = grow(growTum, tumStatCC)

Keep the very intense areas only where they touch a hyper-intense one. Then grow once more into the regions whose texture is similar to the tumour.

The texture similarity is a statistical operator, not a logical one.

How accurate is accurate?

The usual score is the Dice–Sørensen coefficient between the computed region \(X\) and the reference \(Y\):

\( \mathrm{Dice}(X, Y) = \dfrac{2\,|X \cap Y|}{|X| + |Y|} \)

1 is a perfect overlap, 0 is no overlap. Eighteen methods of the BraTS 2017 challenge scored 0.88 on average, from 0.64 to 0.96.

In BraTS the reference is drawn by up to four human raters and merged by a voting rule.

A score of 0.97 means that the method reproduces how a few people drew a boundary, as merged by a rule. Different experts draw different boundaries.

Metrics, revisited

Unsuitable algorithms could be wrongly regarded as the best-performing ones.

A. Reinke, …, L. Maier-Hein. Understanding metric-related pitfalls in image analysis validation. Nature Methods 21, 2024.

With a readable procedure one can ask what a score measures: which part of the boundary, which rule of the rater. A score alone does not say.

Logic and neural networks

A procedure in the logic

  • Can be read, discussed, checked against guidelines.
  • Needs no training data: a new idea can be tried at once.
  • Does not learn from examples, and does not generalise by itself.

A neural network

  • Learns from annotated examples.
  • nnU-Net configures itself on a new dataset; it is a standard reference for segmentation.
  • Cannot be read: its rule is in its weights.

F. Isensee, P. F. Jaeger, S. A. A. Kohl, J. Petersen, K. H. Maier-Hein. nnU-Net. Nature Methods 18, 2021.

A program is a graph of tasks

Maximal sharing, one level up: the nodes are now training runs, predictions and whole datasets.

The graph is evaluated lazily, its results are stored, and independent tasks run in parallel.

Different nodes can run in different places, close to the data: a hospital need not send its images away.

Is the computation deterministic?

A cache keyed on the syntax assumes that the same expression always has the same value.

touch(a, b), percentiles(img, mask, 0.5): same arguments, same result. Storing it is safe.

train(dataset): two runs give two different networks. Which one is "the value"? The one computed first, the one computed here, or train(model, date, place)?

This is a question about the semantics of the language, and the design of the cache depends on its answer.

What stays

  • The language: programs accepted by 2.0 run with the same meaning and give the same values.
  • Every primitive, nnU-Net training and prediction included.
  • The library of VoxLogicA 1, as the namespace vox1.

What changes

  • The scheduler, the workers and the store are written in Rust.
  • The image operations stay in Python, on free-threaded Python, so they run on many threads at once.
  • The part of the scheduler that decides what runs next is generated from a formal model.

Status: release candidate; a pre-release was tagged on 12 November 2026.

From source to graph

  • The parser tables are generated from the grammar of VoxLogicA 2.0, so the two versions read the same language.
  • Names are resolved; user functions are inlined; loop variables become indices.
  • Identical sub-expressions become one node, as in VoxLogicA 1.
  • Errors are reported before any computation, at the call in the source.

Type checking is abstract execution

The type checker walks the same graph the engine executes, in the same order, with types in place of values.

enginetype checker
evaluatesnode → valuenode → type
per nodethe primitive's codethe primitive's type rule
loop bodiesonce per elementonce, with the loop variable bound to a typed hole
starts fromthe goalsthe goals

Nothing is checked that the engine would not run. for x in range(0,5) do [x, x] has type sequence(sequence(int)).

Lazy, and stored

Only what a goal needs

A program is a set of declarations. Nothing is computed until a print or save asks for it.

An if computes only the branch its condition chooses. Asking for one element of a loop computes that element alone.

Values named by what computed them

Each value is named by a hash of the expression that computed it and of the content of its input files. A later run reads stored values instead of recomputing them.

When an input file changes, everything computed from it is computed again.

A scheduler generated from a model

The deciding part of the engine chooses which node to create, which primitive to start, what to keep and when a goal is delivered. It is generated from a model in mCRL2, a process algebra with a model checker.

no deadlockuntil the run ends, some step is always possible
goals deliveredevery goal ends computed or reported as failed
program ordergoals are delivered in the order the program writes them
boundedthe number of live evaluations does not grow with the number of cases
sharingnothing is computed twice

Each property is checked on the model. Each one also has a small defect introduced on purpose, which the property must catch.

Limits of the guarantee

  • The properties are checked on a set of reference programs, with loops over up to 8 cases nested up to 4 deep. Deeper programs are tested, not proved.
  • They cover the deciding part. A primitive that never returns, or a library that crashes, is outside the model; the engine watches for those while it runs.
  • The bound counts evaluations, not bytes. Memory is handled by reading it while the program runs.

How 2.1 was built

Most of the new engine was written by AI coding agents, under a project tracker called NAIMA, made for this kind of work: requirements, tests, metrics and review gates, with the agents doing the work and a person deciding.

NAIMA: naima-project.github.io

  • On 5 October 2026 the plan of the reimplementation was written in the tracker: the owner's decisions, the phases, the work items and their dependencies.
  • Each milestone is a gate that holds when a runnable test proves it.
  • The person states the invariants: memory stays bounded, every node goes through one code path, the scheduler is generated from the model.

The maze in VoxLogicA 2.1


import "simpleitk"
import "vox1"

base = ReadImage("maze.png")

isColor(img, rc, gc, bc) = (red(img) =. rc)
    & (green(img) =. gc) & (blue(img) =. bc)
png(x) = BinaryThreshold(x, 1, 1, 255, 0)

g = isColor(base, 0, 255, 0)
b = isColor(base, 0, 0, 255)
w = isColor(base, 255, 255, 255)

pathToExit = touch(w, g)

print "corridors to the exit" volume(pathToExit)
print "items that can escape" volume(touch(b, pathToExit))
save "maze-v21-1.png" png(pathToExit)

The library of VoxLogicA 1 is imported as vox1; the image is read with a SimpleITK filter. Declarations have no let.

The five saved images are the same sets that VoxLogicA 1 computes.

Full program: code/lesson-11/maze-v21.imgql.

Running it twice


$ voxlogica run maze-v21.imgql --store-db store
corridors to the exit=22902.0
items that can escape=42.0

$ voxlogica run maze-v21.imgql --store-db store
corridors to the exit=22902.0
items that can escape=42.0

The first run computes every node and stores the values. The second run looks up the seven values its goals need, finds all seven in the store, and computes nothing.

Every run writes a report in JSON, voxlogica-out/<time>/report.json: the engine, the libraries and their versions, the number of values looked up and found, and for each goal whether it finished.

An experiment that is still running

Take a task that everyone attacks with machine learning, delineating a brain tumour, and attack it with a readable specification only.

No training and no fitted weights: only the words of the domain, composed in the language.

The work is not published. Today you see the method, which you can reuse for your own language. There are no numbers and no images of the results in this lecture.

The questions, in order

  1. Can the language express it? For each image, let the parameters be chosen knowing the right answer. This is not a method for new images: it measures whether the vocabulary can express a good delineation at all.
  2. Which word is missing? Look at the images where it fails, and ask which operation the language lacks. Add it, and measure again.
  3. Can the parameters be chosen without the answer? This comes last.

Designing the language comes before using it.

How the loop runs

Who does what

The person

Direction and invariants: what must never happen, which failure matters, when a number is suspicious, what the guidelines and the anatomy require.

The agent

Mechanism and measurement: writes the programs in the language, runs them, tabulates, renders the images, proposes the next step.

The agent does not write arbitrary code into the result. It writes expressions in a small language whose primitives a person chose, and whose output a person can look at.

Why the language makes the loop possible

Without a language

An idea costs a training run: hours of computation, a new model, and a result that cannot be read.

With a language

An idea costs a few lines: add an operator, run again, look at the images. The engine recomputes only what changed.

A parameter sweep is itself a program in the language, so a whole experiment can be stored, shared and resumed like any other program.

Anthropic's AI for Science program

The project is supported by Anthropic's AI for Science program: a procedure of hybrid AI with a strong symbolic component, with Claude as a guide.

  • Claude proposes segmentation procedures written in a readable spatial logic.
  • Each procedure comes with a stated rationale, and is subject to the approval of an expert.
  • The resulting mask can be inspected and discussed, which is hard to do with end-to-end networks.

What to take home for your own language

  1. The semantic domain came first: closure spaces, before any syntax.
  2. The core is small, six productions. The words users see are a library written in the language.
  3. What was left out, recursion and loops, bought termination, sharing and parallelism.
  4. The primitives are the words of the domain, so an expert who is not a programmer can read a program.
  5. A readable program lets you ask questions that a score alone cannot answer.
